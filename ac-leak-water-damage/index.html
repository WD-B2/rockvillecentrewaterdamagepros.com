AC Leak Water Damage Repair in Rockville Centre, NY

A clogged condensate line or frozen coil can drip into a ceiling for weeks. We dry what the water reached and tell you what the HVAC tech should fix.

AC leak water damage repair in Rockville Centre covers the drywall, insulation and flooring that an air conditioner soaked. The usual causes are a clogged condensate drain, a cracked drain pan or a coil that froze and then melted.

Our line is open 24/7, and we help most with the water already in the house. An HVAC technician fixes the unit. We dry the building and tell you what the readings show.

Signs your AC is leaking into the house

A brown stain on a ceiling near an air handler, a damp smell around a closet furnace or a puddle under an indoor unit are the usual signs. Water dripping from a vent is another, especially in summer when the system runs most.

Condensate is not perfectly clean. It carries dust and microbes from the coil, so a leak that has run for weeks can leave musty drywall and stained insulation.

Where the water goes

Attic air handlers drip through the ceiling below. Closet units soak the floor and wall base. Basement units run onto the slab and nearby boxes.

Because the leak is often slow, the damage is hidden. A moisture meter and thermal camera find the wet zone before anyone opens a wall.

How we dry and repair the damage

  1. Find the extent of the wet area with meters and thermal imaging.
  2. Remove wet insulation, damaged drywall or soft flooring.
  3. Dry the cavity with air movers and a dehumidifier.
  4. Treat surfaces for mold risk when readings or smell call for it.
  5. Hand the space back ready for patching and paint.

If the stain has reached the ceiling, the repair follows the steps in ceiling water damage repair. If the leak has gone on long enough to leave dark growth, see mold remediation for what New York requires.

Preventing the next leak

  • Have an HVAC technician flush the condensate line each season.
  • Install a float switch that shuts the unit off when the pan fills.
  • Check the drain pan for rust or cracks.
  • Replace filters on schedule so the coil does not ice up.
  • Look at the ceiling near the air handler every few months.

These steps are cheap compared with a ceiling replacement. A float switch costs far less than the repair it can prevent.
