Emergency appliance leak water damage in Rockville Centre usually comes from a dishwasher, washing machine, refrigerator ice line or a sink supply hose. The water often runs under cabinets and flooring, where you cannot see it.

Turn off the appliance, close its supply valve and unplug it only if the outlet is dry. Then call our 24/7 line for extraction and drying.

When an appliance leak is an emergency

A washing machine hose that fails can release gallons per minute. A dishwasher leak is slower, but it runs under cabinets and into the subfloor for hours.

A refrigerator ice maker line can leak for weeks behind the unit before a stain shows. If the floor near a fridge feels soft, treat it as an active leak.

Safety first: do not touch an appliance that is plugged in while you stand in water. Turn off the breaker for the circuit only if you can reach the panel dry, and call 911 for any sparking.

Hidden water under cabinets and floors

Cabinet bases, toe-kicks and laminate flooring act like sponges. Water wicks into particleboard, which swells and does not return to its shape.

A thermal camera shows cool, wet zones behind kickplates, and a moisture meter confirms them. That decides whether the floor can be dried in place or has to be lifted.

How we handle it

  1. Extract standing water and pull the appliance away if it is safe.
  2. Lift loose flooring and remove the toe-kick to reach the cavity.
  3. Dry the subfloor and cabinet bases with directed air.
  4. Replace materials that will not recover, such as swollen particleboard.
  5. Check readings before any new flooring goes down.

A kitchen with a wide area of damage follows the same steps as kitchen water damage restoration, and an upstairs laundry leak that came through a ceiling is covered under ceiling water damage repair.

Where appliance leaks hide

Appliance leaks are often slow, which is why they cause so much hidden harm. A dishwasher door gasket, a washing machine drain hose and a refrigerator ice maker line can all drip for weeks behind a panel or under a floor.

Clues include a soft spot in the floor near the appliance, a musty smell in the cabinet, cupped flooring and a stain on the ceiling below. When you see one, pull the appliance out if it is safe and look at the wall behind and the floor under it.

Shut off the supply valve before moving anything, and unplug the unit if the floor is wet. Photograph what you find before cleanup.
