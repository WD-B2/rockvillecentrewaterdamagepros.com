Musty Smell After Water Damage: Causes and Fixes in Rockville Centre

A lingering musty smell is a clue. Something is still damp, and the odor will not go until it dries.

Written by the Rockville Centre Water Damage Pros team · Published November 12, 2026.

Call (516) 845-9582See all services

A musty smell after water damage usually means moisture remains in a wall, a floor or a pad. Air fresheners hide it for a day, but the cause stays.

The way to remove the smell is to find the wet material, dry or remove it and then clean. This guide explains the usual sources and the steps.

Where the smell comes from

Damp materials give off odors as microbes grow in them. Carpet pad, the bottom of drywall, insulation and wood subfloor are common culprits because they hold water for a long time.

A smell that is stronger in one room, or after rain, points to a specific wet spot. A smell that comes from a vent may mean moisture in ductwork or a nearby wall.

Steps to clear the odor

  • Stop any active leak.
  • Remove materials that cannot be dried, such as wet pad.
  • Dry the structure until readings are normal.
  • Clean surfaces and treat for odor.
  • Improve ventilation or run a dehumidifier.

If the smell comes back, check again for hidden moisture. Mold on drywall over 10 square feet is a job for licensed professionals under New York rules, and mold remediation explains the process.

What not to do

Do not rely on scented sprays, and do not paint over stains. Both cover the sign without fixing the cause.

Do not ignore a persistent smell. If a smell lingers after you have dried and cleaned, ask a professional to take readings.

When the odor returns after cleaning

If a smell comes back, something stayed wet. It might be insulation inside a wall, the back of a baseboard or the subfloor below a repaired surface. Surface cleaning cannot reach those places.

A second round of readings often finds it. If the readings show moisture in a hidden area, the options include opening the area, drying in place and adding ventilation.

Odors can also come from the contents. Upholstery, boxes and clothing that sat wet may need cleaning or disposal. For wet floors, carpet water damage restoration explains when carpet and pad can be saved.

A step-by-step way to track the smell down

  • Close windows and let the room sit for an hour, then enter and note where the smell is strongest.
  • Check walls and baseboards for staining or softness.
  • Lift a corner of the carpet or a floor vent if safe to do so.
  • Look inside cabinets and closets along exterior walls.
  • Check the ceiling below any bathroom or kitchen.
  • Use a moisture meter if you have one, and write down the readings.

If you find a damp area, mark it and photograph it. If you find nothing but the smell persists, a professional moisture survey can find what a visual search misses.

Be patient with your nose. Smells fade as you get used to them, so ask a family member or a friend to walk in and tell you what they notice.

When to worry and when to wait

A faint smell that fades in a day after a cleaning is usually the last of the moisture leaving. A smell that is getting stronger, covers a large area or comes with visible staining deserves attention.

If you have had a leak or flood in the last few weeks, treat any smell as a sign that something is still wet. The cost of checking is small compared with the cost of ignoring it.

If a smell is accompanied by sewage odors or gurgling drains, stay out of the area and call a plumber.

Why professional readings help

A moisture meter reads the water content of a material. A thermal camera shows temperature differences that can point to damp areas. Used together, they let a technician follow moisture behind a wall without tearing it open.

Readings also give a baseline. After drying, the same spots are measured again, and the numbers show whether the problem is solved.

If you cannot find the source yourself, a survey is worth it. The cost is small compared with repeated cleanups that never fix the cause.

A note on prevention

Once the odor is gone, keep it gone. Run a dehumidifier in basements, fix gutters and downspouts so they carry water away from the foundation, and check under sinks and around appliances a few times a year.

A small habit, such as feeling the wall behind a washing machine every month, catches a leak long before it makes a smell.

Standards behind the work

The industry references here are ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage and ANSI/IICRC S520 for mold remediation. In New York, the Department of Labor licenses mold assessors and remediators, so ask any company about the licenses that apply.
