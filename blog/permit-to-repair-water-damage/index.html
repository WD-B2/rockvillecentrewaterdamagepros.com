Do You Need a Permit to Repair Water Damage in Rockville Centre?

In Rockville Centre the building department requires a permit for repairs to residential property. Here is how that plays out after water damage.

Written by the Rockville Centre Water Damage Pros team · Published November 2, 2026.

Call (516) 845-9582See all services

Whether you need a permit to repair water damage depends on the work and the village. In Rockville Centre, the building department says a permit is required for any addition, alteration or repair to a residential property.

Drying equipment and cleanup do not usually need a permit. Rebuilding does, and plumbing and electrical work have their own licensing rules.

What usually needs a permit

......
WorkUsually
Drying and extractionNo permit, since it is mitigation.
Replacing drywall and paintCheck with the building department.
Plumbing repair or replacementOften yes, and by a licensed plumber.
Electrical repairOften yes, and by a licensed electrician.
Structural repairYes.
Roof repairCheck with the building department.

These are patterns, not legal advice. Call the building department for your address before the work starts.

Who does the work

In Rockville Centre, plumbers and electricians need village licenses. Contractors who do home improvement work in Nassau County need a license from the Nassau County Office of Consumer Affairs.

Ask for license numbers in writing and check them. An unlicensed contractor can leave you with work that fails inspection and has to be redone.

Why a permit helps you

A permit means an inspector looks at the work. That protects you if the house is sold later, since open permits and unpermitted work can slow a sale.

It also affects insurance. A claim for later damage may be questioned if the earlier repair was done without a permit. Read burst pipe water damage for a case where the plumber and drying crew work together.

Who to call

  • Rockville Centre building department, 110 Maple Avenue, (516) 678-9247.
  • Malverne building department, (516) 599-1200, extensions 113 and 114.
  • East Rockaway building department, (516) 887-6310.
  • Hewlett Bay Park building department, (516) 295-1400.
  • Town of Hempstead Department of Buildings, for unincorporated areas such as Oceanside.

Each village sets its own rules, so the right office depends on your address.

How to ask the building department the right question

Call before the work starts, not after. Give your address, say what failed and describe the planned repair. Ask whether a permit is required, who must pull it and what inspections follow.

Write down the name of the person you speak with and the date. If the answer is that no permit is needed, ask for that in writing by email if possible. It can matter later at resale.

If a contractor says they will handle the permit, confirm it is in the scope. Some bids leave it to the owner, and fees are separate from labor.

What happens when work was done without a permit

Unpermitted work can surface at sale, when a buyer's attorney or the title search asks for certificates. It can also surface after a later claim. Fixing it after the fact often means opening finished walls for inspection.

That is why it is cheaper to do it right the first time. A permit for a drywall and plumbing repair is small next to the cost of reopening a wall.

Mitigation steps such as extraction and drying are usually not construction. If you are drying a flooded basement, basement water damage cleanup explains what happens before the rebuild, and the permit question comes after the structure is dry.

How permits fit into the order of work

The sequence for most losses is the same. First stop the source, then extract water, then dry the structure, then decide what must be rebuilt. The permit question belongs at that last step, once you know what is being replaced.

Plumbing and electrical repairs come earlier, because the source has to be fixed before drying can finish. Those trades carry their own licensing rules, and their work may need an inspection before walls are closed.

Tell your drying contractor which walls will be opened for a plumber. Coordinating avoids covering something an inspector still needs to see.

After inspection, new drywall, paint and flooring go in. Keep the sign-off papers with your other records.

Sample timeline for a burst pipe repair

......
StageWho is involved
Shut off water and call for helpHomeowner
Extract water and set up dryingRestoration crew
Repair the pipeLicensed plumber, with a permit where required
Inspection of plumbing workBuilding department
Confirm the structure is dryRestoration crew
Rebuild walls, floor and paintLicensed contractor

The exact steps depend on your village and on the work. Use this as a rough guide and confirm with the building department.

Keeping your records straight

Keep a folder for the project. Put in it the permit, the inspection results, the contractor's license information, invoices and photos. When you sell or insure the house, the folder answers most questions.

If a permit is closed, ask for a copy of the final sign-off. If it is still open months later, ask the building department what is needed to close it. Open permits can slow a sale.

Contractors sometimes ask the owner to pull the permit. That puts responsibility on you, so understand what it involves and ask the building department before agreeing.

When in doubt, ask

Rules change, and they differ between villages. The safest rule is to ask the building department before work begins and to write down the answer.

A short call costs nothing, and it can prevent a long delay later. Bring your address and a simple description of the repair.

Safety steps before repair work starts

  • Stay out of rooms where water is near outlets, cords or the electrical panel.
  • Shut off the water at the main valve if a pipe or fixture is the source.
  • Do not walk on a sagging ceiling or a bowed floor.
  • Keep children and pets away from standing water.
  • Take photos before moving anything, then call.

Standards behind the work

The industry reference for this kind of work is ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard for professional water damage restoration. Whoever you hire, ask how it applies to your job and ask to see the moisture readings that show the structure is dry.

What this means in Rockville Centre

Rockville Centre is a village with its own building department, while Oceanside is an unincorporated hamlet that goes through the Town of Hempstead. A repair that crosses a boundary can fall under different offices.

The Nassau County Office of Consumer Affairs, at (516) 571-2600, can confirm a contractor's license. If a repair follows a flood, see flood damage cleanup, and our crews work in Oceanside and Malverne as well.
