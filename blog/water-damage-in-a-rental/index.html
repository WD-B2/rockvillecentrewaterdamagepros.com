Water Damage in a Rental: What Tenants and Landlords Do in Rockville Centre

Speed and a paper trail matter most. Tenants should notify in writing, and landlords should start the drying.

Written by the Rockville Centre Water Damage Pros team · Published November 9, 2026.

Call (516) 845-9582See all services

Water damage in a rental is stressful for both sides. The tenant wants a safe home and the landlord wants to protect the building. Acting fast and keeping written records helps both.

This guide covers the first steps, how responsibility is usually divided and what to document. It is general information and not legal advice.

First steps for a tenant

  • Stop the source if you safely can, for example by closing a fixture valve.
  • Move belongings out of the wet area.
  • Photograph and video the damage.
  • Notify the landlord or manager in writing the same day.
  • Keep a copy of every message.

If there is any risk of electrical hazard, stay out of the area and call the landlord or an emergency line.

First steps for a landlord

Stop the source, call a plumber if a pipe failed and start drying right away. Delay turns a small repair into a larger one because wet drywall and carpet pad can grow mold within days.

Document the cause and the scope before anything is removed. Photos and moisture readings help later with insurance and with any dispute over responsibility.

Responsibility depends on the lease, the cause and the law. Rent abatement or a temporary move may be part of the discussion. For an exact answer, speak with an attorney.

Why drying cannot wait

Water that is not removed spreads into walls and floors. Quick work from a water heater leak cleanup crew or a similar team limits what has to be removed and lowers the cost for whoever pays.

Multi-unit buildings add another risk, since water moves between floors. A leak in one unit can damage the ceiling of the one below.

A paper trail that protects both sides

Tenants and landlords both benefit from clear records. Dated photos, written notice and a log of calls show that the problem was reported and handled. Text messages and emails count if they are kept.

Landlords should keep invoices from the plumber, the drying company and any rebuild contractor. Tenants should keep receipts for emergency expenses such as a hotel or laundering, along with a list of damaged belongings.

If an insurance claim is filed, each party's insurer will ask for these records. A clean file speeds up the process.

Common situations in apartments and rented houses

....
SituationFirst step
Leak from the unit aboveNotify the landlord and the neighbor, photograph the ceiling.
Water heater leaking in a basement unitShut off supply if safe, notify the landlord.
Storm water through a windowMove belongings, notify the landlord, photograph.
Sewer backupStay out of the area, notify the landlord and call for help.

In each case, written notice comes first, and cleanup should start quickly. A landlord who delays raises their own cost, because wet materials spread damage.

Insurance for tenants and landlords

A landlord's property insurance generally covers the building, such as walls, floors and fixtures. It does not cover a tenant's belongings. A tenant needs renter's insurance to protect clothing, furniture and electronics.

Renter's policies often also pay for temporary housing if the unit is not livable, subject to the terms. Check yours for additional living expense coverage.

Flood is typically excluded from both types of policy. A separate flood policy is needed for that risk, and the National Flood Insurance Program is one place where such coverage is available.

Read the lease for clauses about notice, access and who handles repairs. If you are a tenant and you have no renter's insurance, consider getting it before the next loss.

When the damage is serious

If the unit is unsafe, leave and tell the landlord in writing. Discuss how long repairs will take, whether rent will be adjusted and where you can stay.

Landlords should also think about neighbors. Water that moves through floors can reach other units, so check below and beside the affected one.

Communicating calmly and clearly

These situations get tense, but clear and polite communication helps everyone. Tenants should state what happened, when they noticed it and what they have done so far. Landlords should state when help is coming and what the plan is.

Put dates in every message. Avoid blaming language, and stick to facts. If you need to discuss rent, repairs or relocation, ask for a time to talk and confirm the agreement in writing afterward.

If you think your health or safety is at risk, leave the area and seek advice. Your safety matters more than any dispute.

Prevention for rentals

Both sides can lower the odds of a loss. Landlords can inspect supply lines, water heaters and roofs on a schedule. Tenants can report drips and stains early, avoid leaving tubs or washers unattended and know where the shutoff valve is.

A small report today is cheaper than a ceiling that collapses next month.
