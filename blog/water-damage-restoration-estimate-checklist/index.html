What a Water Damage Restoration Estimate Should Include in Rockville Centre

A good estimate lets you compare two bids on the same terms. Here is what belongs in one.

Written by the Rockville Centre Water Damage Pros team · Published October 26, 2026.

Call (516) 845-9582See all services

A water damage restoration estimate is only useful if you can compare it with another one. That requires a clear scope, quantities and a plain statement of what is not included.

We do not publish a flat price here because every loss differs. What we can do is show you what a well-built estimate contains, so you can spot gaps.

Start with the scope

The estimate should name the rooms affected, the water category and the cause if it is known. It should describe the work in order: extraction, demolition, drying, cleaning and any rebuild.

Look for square footage and linear footage. Without quantities, you cannot tell whether two bids cover the same work.

Line items worth looking for

........
ItemWhat it tells you
Water categoryClean, gray or black water sets the handling steps.
ExtractionThe method and the area covered.
DemolitionWhich materials come out and how much.
Drying equipmentThe number of air movers and dehumidifiers and the days planned.
MonitoringWhether readings are taken and shared.
Cleaning and treatmentAntimicrobial or odor steps where needed.
ContentsHandling of belongings, if any.
RebuildWhether it is included or separate.

Equipment is often billed per unit per day, so ask how the days will be decided. The right answer is based on moisture readings, not a fixed number.

What a fair estimate admits

Water damage scope can change when walls are opened. A good estimate says so, and explains how added work will be approved.

It also lists exclusions, such as repairs to the pipe, roof or electrical work. Those belong to other trades, and an honest bid names them.

Red flags

  • A single lump sum with no scope.
  • No mention of water category.
  • A promise to start work before any inspection.
  • Pressure to sign today.
  • No explanation of how drying will be measured.

Promises of exact prices over the phone are a warning sign. A responsible company explains the factors and quotes after seeing the loss. Our structural drying page shows what equipment and monitoring look like.

How to compare two estimates fairly

Lay the two estimates side by side and match them room by room. Mark any line that appears in one but not the other. Those gaps explain most price differences.

Then compare the drying plan. Count air movers, dehumidifiers and planned days. If one bid has twice the equipment, ask why. The answer should refer to the size of the area and the materials wet.

Check what each estimate assumes about the rebuild. One may stop at dry and clean, while another includes new drywall and paint. They are not the same job and should not be compared as if they were.

Questions to ask before you accept

  • Is the water source stopped, and who confirms it?
  • What happens if more damage is found behind a wall?
  • How will I see moisture readings?
  • What are the payment terms and when are they due?
  • Does the estimate cover removal and disposal of debris?
  • Who is responsible for permits?

A company that welcomes these questions is a better choice than one that avoids them. A written answer protects both sides.

Estimates, authorizations and invoices: three different papers

An estimate is a prediction. An authorization is your signed permission to start work, and it may include terms about payment and insurance. An invoice is the bill. They are not the same, and each deserves a read.

Check that the authorization does not give the contractor control over your insurance claim without your understanding. Some forms assign benefits, and you should know what that means before you sign.

Check the invoice against the estimate. Each increase should map to a signed change order or documented extra work. If it does not, ask for an explanation.

Keep all three together in the same folder, along with the readings log and photos.

Payment and timing

Ask when payment is due and how. Many restoration jobs are billed to insurance, but you are still responsible for your deductible and anything the insurer does not cover. Clarify who the contractor will bill.

Be wary of large upfront cash payments before work begins. A reasonable arrangement ties payment to progress and documentation.

Taking notes during the walkthrough

When the estimator visits, walk with them and take notes. Ask them to point out each wet area and say what they plan to do about it. Photograph the areas they mark.

Ask them to explain anything you do not understand, such as a flood cut or a drying goal. A good estimator likes the chance to explain, and the answers help you compare bids later.

After the visit, ask for the written estimate within a day. If it takes longer, ask when to expect it, and keep water out of the house in the meantime.

When the estimate changes

A fair estimate can change for good reasons, such as hidden damage found behind a wall. The question is how the change is handled. It should come with a reason, a photo and a written update before the work goes ahead.

If the change comes after the work is done, with no warning, ask for the documentation and take your time reviewing it.
