Emergency basement water damage cleanup in Rockville Centre starts with whether the water is clean, gray or dirty. A pipe leak adds clean water, a washer or sump overflow adds gray water and a sewer backup or storm flood adds contaminated water.

Stay off the stairs if water is near the panel or outlets. Our 24/7 line helps you decide what is safe, and we pump, remove ruined materials and dry the space.

When a flooded basement is an emergency

Water in a basement is an emergency because it is likely to reach electrical equipment, the furnace and the water heater. Anything stored on the floor is at risk, and water pressure against walls can push more through cracks.

Do not go downstairs if water is above the first step or reaching outlets. Call 911 or the utility if you see sparks, smell gas or hear a hum from the panel.

Safety first: basement water can be energized. Do not wade in until power is off by a safe means, and do not carry a space heater or fan into the water.

Where basement water comes from

Heavy rain, a failed sump pump, a cracked foundation, a floor drain backup and a broken pipe are the main sources. The cause decides the water category and the cleanup plan.

A sump pump that loses power during a storm is a classic failure. A battery backup, or a check of the pump before the season, can prevent a repeat.

Basements on the South Shore

Houses in Rockville Centre commonly have basements, and many are finished with carpet, drywall and storage. On September 29, 2023, the National Weather Service recorded more than six inches in nearby Hewlett and almost eight in East Rockaway, and basements across the area flooded.

If the water has backed up through a drain, treat it as sewage and see sewage backup cleanup. For water that arrived with a storm, flood damage cleanup covers the outside-water steps.

How we clean up a basement

  1. Pump out standing water and extract what remains.
  2. Remove wet carpet, pad and drywall that cannot be saved.
  3. Move and sort stored items, discarding what cannot be dried.
  4. Clean and disinfect surfaces, then dry the space.
  5. Check readings and, where needed, recommend a mold assessment.

Concrete walls and floors dry slowly, so humidity control matters in a basement. Dehumidifiers run until the air and the slab reach a normal level.

Sump pumps, floor drains and what to check first

Many basement floods start with a device that did not work. A sump pump with a stuck float, a failed backup battery or a clogged discharge line cannot keep up with a storm. Floor drains can back up when the sewer is overloaded.

After the water is out, test the pump by pouring water into the pit and watching it start and shut off. Check that the discharge line runs away from the foundation and is not frozen or clogged.

If your basement flooded from sewage, the cleaning steps differ, so call and tell us what you see.

Safety steps before help arrives

  • Stay out of rooms where water is near outlets, cords or the electrical panel.
  • Shut off the water at the main valve if a pipe or fixture is the source.
  • Do not walk on a sagging ceiling or a bowed floor.
  • Keep children and pets away from standing water.
  • Take photos before moving anything, then call.

Standards behind the work

The industry reference for this kind of work is ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard for professional water damage restoration. Whoever you hire, ask how it applies to your job and ask to see the moisture readings that show the structure is dry.
