Wall and Drywall Water Damage Repair in Rockville Centre

Drywall holds water like a sponge. We find how far it goes and dry or remove what has to go.

Call (516) 845-9582See all services

Wall and drywall water damage repair in Rockville Centre starts with the cause. Drywall damaged by a leak, a roof drip or a plumbing break swells, softens and stains. Left wet, it grows mold.

Call our 24/7 line if a wall is wet, bubbling or leaning out. We measure the moisture, remove what has failed and dry the cavity before anyone closes it up.

Signs of wet drywall

Brown or yellow stains, bubbling paint, a soft spot when you press and seams that open are the common signs. A musty smell at the base of a wall also points to water inside.

Drywall wicks upward from the floor and spreads sideways from a leak. The stain on the surface is smaller than the wet area inside.

Dry it or cut it out

Drywall can sometimes be dried in place if the water was clean and the response was fast. Once it has softened, crumbled or grown mold, it is cut out and replaced.

How we handle a wall

  1. Find the source and stop it.
  2. Map the moisture with a meter and thermal camera.
  3. Cut a flood cut, usually 2 feet above the water line, if the cavity is wet.
  4. Dry the framing and remaining board with air movers and a dehumidifier.
  5. Confirm readings, then hand off for patching, priming and paint.

Ceilings follow similar steps, covered in ceiling water damage repair. If a wall is wet from a leak in a bathroom or kitchen, burst pipe water damage explains what the plumber and the dry-out crew each do.

Walls in Rockville Centre homes

Older houses in the village often have plaster over lath, which is heavier and slower to dry. A stain on plaster may need a different repair than on drywall.

Wall repairs of any size need a contractor with a Nassau County Home Improvement License, and the building department at (516) 678-9247 can tell you whether a permit applies. Ask before you sign.

Cut it or dry it

Drywall can sometimes be dried in place if the water was clean, the exposure was short and the board is firm. Soft, crumbly or stained board, or board that touched dirty water, is removed.

Insulation inside the wall is another factor. Wet batt insulation holds water against the studs and usually comes out. Closed-cell foam may be left.

A flood cut, which removes the lower portion of the board, exposes the cavity. After drying, new board is hung, taped and finished. Ask whether that rebuild is part of the scope or a separate trade.

Standards behind the work

The industry reference for this kind of work is ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard for professional water damage restoration. Whoever you hire, ask how it applies to your job and ask to see the moisture readings that show the structure is dry.
