Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Rockville Centre, NY

Gurgling drains, toilets that will not flush and dirty water in a basement drain are signs of a backup. Stay out of the water and call.

Emergency sewage backup cleanup in Rockville Centre begins with a rule: do not touch the water. Sewage contains bacteria and viruses, and contact with skin or breathing the spray is not safe.

Keep people and pets out of the room, stop using water in the house and call our 24/7 line. We tell you what to do, and a trained crew removes the water and disinfects what remains.

Signs your sewer line is backing up

Several fixtures draining slowly at once, gurgling in a floor drain, toilets that bubble when the washer runs and dirty water in a basement drain are classic signs. A smell of sewage in the basement is another.

A backup can come from a blocked house line, tree roots, a collapsed pipe or a surcharged municipal line after heavy rain. The cause decides who fixes it, and it is the first question for a plumber.

Sewage backup: what to do first. Keep everyone out, do not flush or run water, do not use fans or a vacuum on the water and wash well if you touch it. If power outlets are wet, call 911 or the utility before the cleanup line.

Why this is a Category 3 loss

The S500 standard classes sewage as Category 3, or black water. Porous materials that it touched, such as carpet, pad and drywall bottoms, are usually removed rather than cleaned.

Hard surfaces can be cleaned and disinfected. A crew decides what stays, based on how long the water sat and what it touched.

Sewers in Rockville Centre and nearby

Rockville Centre is one of six municipal sewer districts in Nassau County that pump to county treatment plants. A blocked line inside your house is your plumber, while trouble in the street line is a village matter.

The Bay Park plant in East Rockaway serves about 40 percent of the county, roughly 550,000 people, and was destroyed by Superstorm Sandy. Storms still stress the system, so heavy-rain backups are a real possibility on the South Shore.

How cleanup proceeds

  1. Crews wear protective gear and contain the area.
  2. Water is pumped and extracted to approved disposal.
  3. Porous materials that touched sewage are removed.
  4. Surfaces are cleaned, disinfected and dried.
  5. Readings confirm the area is dry before any rebuild.

Basement backups often overlap with basement water damage cleanup. If the cause was a storm, see flood damage cleanup.

Why sewage cleanup is not a mop-up job

Sewage water is treated as black water, which means it can carry bacteria and other contaminants. Porous materials that touched it, such as carpet, pad and drywall, are usually removed. Hard surfaces are cleaned and treated.

Crews wear protective equipment, and the area is isolated so that contamination does not spread. Sealing HVAC returns in the affected zone is part of the plan.

If the backup began at a floor drain or a toilet, a plumber may need to clear the line. Cleanup cannot be finished until the source stops.
