Frozen pipes split quietly and release the water when they thaw. If a pipe has cracked, close the main valve and call us.

Emergency frozen pipe water damage in Rockville Centre often shows up as a surprise. The pipe splits while it is frozen, and the flood starts only when the ice melts and water pushes through the crack.

If your faucets stopped running on a freezing night, or you see frost on a pipe, shut off the main water valve before the thaw. Our 24/7 line helps with the water, the drying and the next steps.

When a frozen pipe becomes an emergency

A pipe that is frozen but not split is a warning. A pipe that is bulging, cracked or leaking as it warms is an active flood. Treat any leak after a freeze as urgent, because supply lines run under pressure and can empty into a wall cavity.

Do not use an open flame or a torch to thaw a pipe. A hair dryer on low heat, applied while a faucet stays open, is the safer method, and a plumber should check the pipe afterward.

Safety first: if water is dripping from a ceiling fixture or near a panel, do not touch the fixture. Turn off the breaker only if you can reach it dry, and call 911 or the utility about any shock risk.

Where pipes freeze in a Rockville Centre home

The usual spots are exterior walls, uninsulated crawl spaces, garages, attics and runs near a basement window. A hard cold snap after a mild week is enough to push an exposed line past freezing.

Nassau County gets freeze-thaw cycles through the winter, which means a pipe can freeze, thaw and refreeze. A line that survived last winter is not proof the same spot is safe this one.

What we do after the thaw

We extract the water, trace where it traveled and open the wall only if readings call for it. A split pipe often wets the ceiling below it, so we check both sides of the floor.

Drying focuses on insulation, subfloor and the bottom of the wall. After the plumber repairs the pipe, we confirm the cavity is dry before it is closed. The steps for the water itself are the same as in burst pipe water damage calls.

Preventing a repeat

  • Insulate pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls.
  • Let a faucet drip on the coldest nights if a line runs through an exposed wall.
  • Keep cabinet doors open under sinks on outside walls during a freeze.
  • Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses before winter.
  • Know where your main shutoff valve is and test that it turns.

If your house stands empty for a stretch in winter, someone should check it. A split pipe in an empty house can run for days before anyone notices.
