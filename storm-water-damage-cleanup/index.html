Emergency storm water damage cleanup in Rockville Centre covers water that came in with a nor'easter, a tropical system or a heavy summer downpour. It may come through the roof, around windows, through a bulkhead or up from the ground.

During storms our line stays open 24/7, though busy nights can slow a response. Cover openings if it is safe, shut off power to wet areas if you can reach the panel dry and call us.

When storm water is an emergency

Water entering a house during a storm is an emergency, even if the rain slows. Roof openings let in more with each squall, and wet insulation drops into ceilings.

Outside water is treated as contaminated, so carpet and soft materials may need removal. Stay out of floodwater that could hide wires or debris.

Safety first: if a tree or wire is down, stay away and call 911 or the utility. Do not go onto a wet roof, and do not use outlets in rooms where water is dripping.

Storm history on the South Shore

On September 29, 2023, East Rockaway recorded 7.90 inches of rain and Hewlett 6.34 inches according to the National Weather Service, and JFK set a record at 8.05 inches. A Malverne reporting station logged 7.48 inches. Streets, basements and drains across the area took on water in hours.

Superstorm Sandy left a longer mark. Water spread through neighborhoods across the South Shore and damaged the Bay Park plant in East Rockaway. If you live near the water or on low ground, plan for storms.

How storm cleanup proceeds

  1. Temporarily cover roof or wall openings so more rain stays out.
  2. Extract standing water.
  3. Remove wet insulation, ceiling material and flooring that cannot be dried.
  4. Dry the structure and measure moisture.
  5. Document the damage for your insurance claim.

Roof repair is done by a roofer, and a temporary cover is often the first step. Ground-level flooding follows flood damage cleanup, and a flooded lower level uses basement water damage cleanup.

Storm damage and your policy

Wind-driven rain through a damaged roof is treated differently than rising flood water. Standard policies often cover the first and not the second, and flood needs its own coverage.

Take photos of damage, save receipts for temporary repairs and ask your insurer what is required. The New York Department of Financial Services notes that photographs before cleanup help your claim.

After the storm passes: a first-day plan

  • Check for downed lines and stay away from wet electrical equipment.
  • Photograph exterior damage and interior water.
  • Cover openings with tarps if it is safe.
  • Move belongings to dry places.
  • Call your insurer and your restoration company.

Storm days overload crews, so call early. Tell us where the water entered, such as a window, roof or foundation, because that changes the drying plan.
