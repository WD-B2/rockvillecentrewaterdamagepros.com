About Rockville Centre Water Damage Pros

A 24/7 water damage restoration line serving Rockville Centre and the nearby South Shore.

Call (516) 845-9582See all services

Rockville Centre Water Damage Pros is a water damage restoration line based at 308 Sunrise Highway in Rockville Centre, New York. We take emergency calls around the clock for water extraction, drying and cleanup, and we serve the village and nearby South Shore towns.

What we focus on

We focus on one problem, which is getting water out of a building and getting the structure dry. That covers burst pipes, flooded basements, storm water, appliance leaks, ceiling leaks and sewer backups. We also help with the cleanup that follows, including wet carpet, hardwood floors and drywall.

Mold is a secondary topic for us. We explain how New York licensing works and we point you to the right professionals, because mold work over 10 square feet requires a licensed assessor and a separate licensed remediator.

What happens when you call

  • A person answers the 24/7 line.
  • We ask your address, where the water is coming from and whether anyone is at risk.
  • We give you safety steps for the first hour, such as shutting off water or staying out of a room.
  • We explain the scope and what affects the price before work starts.
  • Drying continues until moisture readings show the structure is dry.

We do not use contact forms. A phone call is faster in an emergency, and it lets us ask the questions that matter while you are still at the property.

How we think about the work

Water damage is a race against time. Wet drywall, pad and wood begin to break down within a day or two, and mold becomes a real possibility after that. The first hours decide how much has to be removed and how much can be dried in place.

That is why we put extraction and drying first and rebuilding last. A new wall built over a wet cavity is a future problem. We would rather measure moisture, explain the readings and tell you when the structure is ready for repair.

We also try to be plain about limits. Some jobs need a plumber first, or an electrician, or a licensed mold professional. When that is the case, we say so rather than promising to handle everything.

Why local knowledge matters here

Rockville Centre sits on the South Shore of Nassau County. The village runs its own water system from wells, has older neighborhoods such as Strathmore and sits close to areas that flood in big storms. On September 29, 2023, the National Weather Service recorded 7.90 inches of rain in East Rockaway and 6.34 inches in Hewlett, a reminder that basements here can fill quickly.

Building rules also differ by place. Rockville Centre requires a permit for any addition, alteration or repair to residential property, while Oceanside works through the Town of Hempstead and Malverne and East Rockaway run their own village building departments. Knowing which office to call saves time after a loss.

The three kinds of water we plan around

Clean water comes from a supply line or a faucet, and it is the easiest to deal with if it is caught fast. Gray water comes from washing machines, dishwashers and similar sources, and it carries more contamination. Black water comes from sewers and storm flooding, and it requires protective steps and the removal of porous materials.

Knowing which kind you have changes everything about the plan, from the gear a crew wears to whether carpet can be saved. When you call, describe where the water came from and we will ask the right follow-up questions.

Homes, apartments and businesses

Houses are the most common calls, but apartments and small commercial spaces matter just as much. In a building with several units, water moves between floors, so the first question is often where else it has gone. Shops and offices add another worry, which is how long the space must stay closed.

Whatever the building, the steps are the same. Stop the source, remove standing water, dry the structure and confirm it with readings. What changes is the schedule, the access and the people who need to be told.

The standards we measure against

The industry reference for water damage work is ANSI/IICRC S500, and for mold it is ANSI/IICRC S520. We cite them so you know what to ask any company, including us. Ask how the standard applies to your job, and ask to see moisture readings that show the structure is dry.

What we do not claim

You will not find invented awards, made-up reviews or promises of exact arrival times on this site. Every situation is different, and the honest answer to most questions starts with a look at the damage. We would rather earn your trust on the phone than on a banner.
