A musty smell, dark spots or growth after a leak mean moisture is still present. Fix the water first, then follow New York mold rules.

Mold remediation in Rockville Centre follows a simple order. Fix the moisture, assess the growth and remove it under New York rules. Removing mold without stopping the water only brings it back.

Call our 24/7 line if you see growth, smell something musty or have had water for more than a day or two. We explain the process and what has to be done by licensed professionals.

Signs you may have mold after water damage

A musty smell, dark or fuzzy spots on drywall, ceiling tiles or wood and paint that peels in a wet area are common signs. Mold often appears behind furniture, under carpet or inside a wall after a hidden leak.

Mold needs moisture, so a spot that keeps returning points to an active water source. A moisture meter and thermal camera look for that source.

What New York requires

The New York State Department of Labor licenses mold assessors and remediators under Article 32 for jobs over 10 square feet. The assessor and remediator must be separate parties, which keeps the person who writes the plan from profiting from the cleanup.

For small areas under 10 square feet, a homeowner may handle the work, but the moisture source still has to be fixed. We can tell you which category your job falls into and what is allowed.

Steps in a remediation

  1. Stop the water and dry the area.
  2. A licensed assessor inspects and writes a plan for larger jobs.
  3. A licensed remediator contains the area and removes affected materials.
  4. Surfaces are cleaned with HEPA vacuums and wiped.
  5. A post-work check confirms the area is ready for rebuild.

Industry standards such as ANSI/IICRC S520 describe how mold remediation is approached. We name it as a reference for the process, not as a claim about any certification. After the area is dry, structural drying confirms readings are normal.

Mold and water damage in Rockville Centre homes

Houses in the village with finished basements, older plaster and attics are common places for hidden moisture. After a storm, a basement that stays damp for several days is at risk.

If you have standing or recent water, start with water extraction so the moisture does not feed growth. Our guides on mold in a house, a bathroom or an attic explain what homeowners can do and when to call a professional.

What to do while you wait for an assessor

  • Stop any water source that feeds the area.
  • Keep the room dry and run a dehumidifier.
  • Stay out of the affected space if you can.
  • Do not disturb or scrub large areas.
  • Take photos for your records.

Do not try to seal the area with paint or caulk, which only hides the problem. If a leak is active, call a plumber and a water damage crew first.
