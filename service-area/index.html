How coverage works

One phone number covers the whole area. When you call, a person asks for your address, the source of the water and whether anyone is at risk. If we can reach you, we tell you what to do in the first few minutes and plan extraction and drying. If we cannot, we say so right away so you can call another company without losing time.

Distances on the South Shore are short, but traffic on Sunrise Highway, Merrick Road and the parkways changes with the hour and the weather. Storm days are the hardest, because many households have water at once. Calling early in a storm helps, since crews can be sent in a sensible order.

What differs from town to town

Each place has its own building office, so permits and inspections after water damage depend on the address. Rockville Centre, Malverne, East Rockaway and the four villages inside Hewlett run their own building departments. Oceanside is an unincorporated hamlet that works through the Town of Hempstead.

Water systems differ too. Rockville Centre runs its own system from wells, while Oceanside is served by New York American Water. Knowing which utility serves your street helps when you need to shut off or ask about a service line.

Housing also varies. Rockville Centre has many older Tudor and Colonial homes, Malverne has homes from several eras, East Rockaway has waterfront areas that flood in storms, and Hewlett has a mix of housing from different periods. Each type fails in its own way, which is why our crews ask about the age and layout of a house when you call.

Valley Stream, Island Park, Elmont and Woodmere also appear in our calls. If you are outside the area listed here, call anyway. We will say honestly whether we can help.

Storms and the South Shore

Heavy storms are the main reason calls pile up. On September 29, 2023, the National Weather Service recorded 9.06 inches in Valley Stream, 7.90 inches in East Rockaway and 6.34 inches in Hewlett, with a gauge near Malverne reading 7.48 inches. Totals like these overwhelm drains, sump pumps and older basements.

East Rockaway has a second reason to take storms seriously. The Bay Park sewage plant, which serves about 40 percent of Nassau County, was destroyed by Superstorm Sandy. Low-lying neighborhoods near the water have a long memory of what flooding means.

If a storm is forecast, move valuables off basement floors, know where your main shutoff is and save our number in your phone. Preparation does not stop water, but it makes the cleanup faster.

We also keep the area map on every page of this site, so you can see at a glance how the towns sit around the village. The map is a guide, not a promise. The honest test of coverage is a phone call at the moment you need us.

For the technical side of the work, the industry reference is ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard for professional water damage restoration. It is a useful yardstick when you compare companies.

Before you call

  • Know your street address and nearest cross street.
  • Note whether the water is clean, from a pipe, or dirty, from a drain or storm.
  • Say if the electrical panel or outlets are wet.
  • Tell us if anyone in the home is elderly, ill or has trouble moving.
  • Photograph the damage if it is safe to do so.

None of this needs to be perfect. If you cannot answer a question, say so and we will walk through it with you. Water damage is stressful, and the person on the line is there to help you take the first steps.
