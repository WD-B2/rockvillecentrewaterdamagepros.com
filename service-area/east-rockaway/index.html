Storm season and flooding in East Rockaway

On September 29, 2023, the National Weather Service recorded 7.90 inches of rain in East Rockaway. That was a one-day total that put water in basements across the village.

Fall and winter bring nor'easters, and summer brings thunderstorms that can drop heavy rain in short bursts. After any of them, check basements and low rooms right away. Our guide to flood damage cleanup explains how outside water is treated.

Why sewage is part of the conversation here

The Bay Park sewage treatment plant lies within East Rockaway. It serves about 40 percent of Nassau County, roughly 550,000 people, and was destroyed by Superstorm Sandy, which led to partially treated sewage being discharged.

That history is a reminder that storm water and sewer systems interact. If dirty water comes up through a basement drain, stay away and call us. See sewage backup cleanup for the steps.

What homeowners here can do before the next storm

  • Know where your main water valve and electrical panel are.
  • Keep valuables and records off basement floors.
  • Test your sump pump if you have one.
  • Photograph your home's condition so you have a baseline.
  • Save our number in your phone.

Safety steps before help arrives

  • Stay out of rooms where water is near outlets, cords or the electrical panel.
  • Shut off the water at the main valve if a pipe or fixture is the source.
  • Do not walk on a sagging ceiling or a bowed floor.
  • Keep children and pets away from standing water.
  • Take photos before moving anything, then call.

Standards behind the work

The industry reference for this kind of work is ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard for professional water damage restoration. Whoever you hire, ask how it applies to your job and ask to see the moisture readings that show the structure is dry.
