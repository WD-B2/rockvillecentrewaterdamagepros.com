Older houses and the water problems that follow

Many Malverne homes were built in the early and middle decades of the last century, with plaster walls, original plumbing in places and basements. Old supply lines corrode, and a failed fitting can leak inside a wall for a long time before a stain appears.

If you see a stain, call early. Our guide to wall and drywall water damage repair explains how we find hidden water, and burst pipe water damage covers what to do when a line fails outright.

Why the age of the house matters in Malverne

Malverne has housing from several eras, and each one fails in its own way. Older homes may have galvanized or early copper lines, plaster or lath walls and original basement walls. Newer homes may have plastic supply lines and finished basements.

Plaster behaves differently from drywall when wet. It may hold up better but it dries more slowly and is harder to repair, so the plan changes.

Village rules that affect a repair

Malverne Village Hall is at 99 Church Street, and the building department can be reached at (516) 599-1200, extensions 113 and 114. Contractors who work in the village must be licensed with the Village.

Plumbing work in Malverne goes through a series of inspections, including rough plumbing, open trench, gas pressure, water test and final. If a plumber is replacing pipe after a leak, ask which inspections apply so the repair is signed off properly.

Village stormwater rules also refer to drywells for new work. Check with the building department before you add drainage after a flood.
