What public records say about Smith Pond

Nassau County hazard mitigation planning for the village lists roadways and park land around Smith Pond among its flood-prone locations. The same plan names residential streets along the Mill River and the Park Avenue Bridge.

The engineering firm WSP describes Smith Pond and the adjacent areas as having repeated historic flooding. Its work included rehabilitating the weir that holds back the pond, using a reinforced concrete encasement and new concrete bulkheads.

The county plan listed a Smith Pond rehabilitation project at about three million dollars, designed for 100 year protection, and a separate drainage study. We cannot confirm the current status of those projects, so check with the village for updates.

What this means for a homeowner

Public records name roads and park land, not individual houses, and we do not claim any particular home has flooded. They do show that heavy rain can overwhelm drainage around the pond.

If your basement or lower level takes on water after a cloudburst, treat it as a flood problem. Flood damage restoration and storm water damage cleanup describe what that work involves.

Storm water can carry dirt and contaminants, so it is handled with more care than a clean pipe leak. We remove the water, discard materials that cannot be cleaned and dry the structure.

If storm water is entering your home

Put your safety first. These steps apply only when you can act without wading through deep water.

  • Stay out of a flooded basement if water is above outlets or near the panel.
  • Never drive or walk through moving flood water on a street.
  • Do not use a household vacuum or a power tool in standing water.
  • Move valuables upstairs while the stairs are dry.
  • Photograph the water level and damaged items for your records.

Flood maps, insurance and the village

The village keeps its flood insurance study and maps at the Building Department, 110 Maple Avenue. The village code sets flood damage prevention rules for properties in mapped special flood hazard areas.

Standard homeowner policies usually exclude flood. Flood coverage is a separate policy and often has a waiting period before it starts, so ask an agent about it well before storm season.

Drying after storm water

After the water is out, the structure still holds moisture. Structural drying and dehumidification explains how air movers and dehumidifiers bring materials back to normal readings.

Damp finishes left in place can grow mold. See mold remediation for the New York rules, including the separate assessor and remediator requirement for projects over 10 square feet.
